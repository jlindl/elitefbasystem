If you are comparing an Amazon Individual vs Professional seller account, the short answer is this: Individual charges you per item sold and suits someone testing the water with a handful of listings, while Professional charges a recurring subscription and is what you need if you plan to sell more than a small number of items a month, use FBA properly, or compete for the Buy Box. Most people who are serious about building an Amazon FBA side business end up on Professional fairly quickly. The actual fee amounts change from time to time, so check the current figures on Amazon's own seller fee pages before you decide, rather than relying on anything you read elsewhere, including this article.

That said, the decision is about more than the fee. It affects which tools you can use, whether your listings can win the Buy Box, and how you look to Amazon from day one. Here is what actually matters.

## Amazon individual vs professional seller account: what changes between the two

An Individual account has no recurring subscription. Instead, Amazon takes a fee per item sold, on top of the usual referral fee. There is no access to bulk listing tools, no advertising, no access to many of the reports you will want once you are running a proper FBA operation, and, critically, no Buy Box eligibility on listings you do not already own outright.

A Professional account has a monthly subscription instead of a per-item fee, and gives you:

- Buy Box eligibility, which matters hugely once you are selling existing products or bundles alongside other sellers
- Bulk listing and inventory tools
- Access to Amazon advertising (Sponsored Products and the rest)
- Access to categories that are gated to Individual sellers
- Reports you need to track sales, returns and advertising performance properly

For anyone planning to use FBA as more than a one-off experiment, Professional is usually the right call almost immediately. If you intend to build bundle listings, run pay-per-click campaigns, or sell in a category that requires approval, Individual will not get you there. The per-item fee can look cheaper on paper if you only expect to sell a few units a month, but it rarely stays that way once a product starts moving, and you cannot win the Buy Box without Professional, which matters as soon as you are not the only seller on a listing.

The practical rule: if you are testing whether you even want to do this, and you are listing one or two items to see how the process feels, Individual is fine for a few weeks. If you already know you want to build a bundle, run ads, or sell more than a trickle of units, go straight to Professional and budget for the subscription as a cost of doing business, in the same way you budget for stock.

## The signup wizard, step by step

Amazon's seller registration wizard asks for the same core information whichever plan you choose:

1. Business details: whether you are registering as a sole trader, limited company or individual, and your registered address
2. Identity documents: a passport or driving licence, and proof of address such as a bank statement
3. Bank account details for payouts
4. A UK billing method for the subscription fee (if you choose Professional)
5. Tax information, including whether you are VAT registered
6. A phone number for verification

None of this is complicated on its own, but the order matters, and small mismatches (a name spelled slightly differently between your ID and your bank statement, for instance) are a common reason verification gets delayed. Have your documents ready and matching before you start, rather than mid-way through the wizard.

## Identity verification and the video call

Amazon verifies every new seller's identity, and for many applicants this now includes a short video call rather than just document upload. You will typically be asked to show your ID on camera, confirm your details verbally, and sometimes show proof of your business address. It is a standard anti-fraud step, not a sign anything has gone wrong, but it does catch people out when:

- The call happens unexpectedly and they are not somewhere quiet with good lighting and their documents to hand
- The name or address on their documents does not match what they entered in the wizard
- They try to join the call from a phone with a poor connection

If you work a 9-5, book time for this rather than hoping to squeeze it into a lunch break. Verification delays are one of the most common reasons a new seller's launch slips by a week or two, and it is entirely avoidable with a bit of planning.

## Day-one account hygiene to avoid a suspension before you have sold anything

It is possible to pick up a suspension or a performance warning before you have shipped a single unit, usually from entirely preventable mistakes. The basics worth getting right from day one:

- Use one email address and one set of business details for your account, and never try to open a second account under different details. Amazon treats this as a serious policy breach.
- Keep your business address, bank details and tax information consistent and up to date. Mismatches trigger reviews.
- Read the category and listing policies for anything you plan to sell before you list it, particularly if it is a branded product, since some categories and brands need approval or supplier invoices.
- Do not copy another seller's images, text or brand name onto your own listing, even informally. That is a trademark and policy problem, not a shortcut.
- Set up two-step verification on your Amazon account and your associated email, since account takeovers are a real and growing risk for new sellers.
- Check your [VAT and tax obligations](/blog/amazon-fba-fees-uk-explained-for-beginners) with HMRC or an accountant early, rather than leaving it until your first sale arrives. Selling on Amazon does not change your underlying tax responsibilities as a UK trader.

## What to do if you only have evenings and weekends

Most people setting up a seller account alongside a 9-5 are doing the whole thing in small windows: registering one evening, gathering ID the next, booking the verification call for a lunch break or a day off, and packing the first boxes on a Sunday once stock has arrived at home. That is a perfectly normal way to build this. A few things make it easier:

- Do the registration and verification in one focused sitting rather than spreading it over several half-attempts, since re-entering the wizard after a timeout wastes an evening.
- Choose Professional from the start if you already know you want to build bundles or run ads, so you are not re-registering or switching plans mid-way through your first launch.
- Keep a simple folder, physical or digital, with your ID, proof of address and bank details, so you are not hunting for documents when a verification request comes in.
- Plan your first stock delivery for a day you are actually home, since a missed delivery while you are at work delays everything that follows.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I switch from Individual to Professional later?

Yes, you can upgrade from an Individual to a Professional account at any point from within Seller Central, and most sellers do this as soon as they are confident they will be listing regularly. There is no need to close your account and start again.

### Do I need a Professional account to use FBA?

You can technically use FBA on an Individual account, but without Professional you cannot access the Buy Box on most listings, cannot run advertising, and cannot use many of the inventory tools that make running FBA properly manageable. For anyone beyond a one-product test, Professional is the realistic choice.

### What documents do I need for the identity verification call?

Typically a valid passport or driving licence and a recent proof of address, such as a bank statement, matching the details you entered during registration. Requirements can vary, so check exactly what Amazon asks for in your specific verification request before the call.

### Will opening a seller account affect my tax position?

Selling on Amazon does not change your underlying obligation to declare income to HMRC as a sole trader or limited company. The rules around VAT registration and reporting depend on your circumstances, so speak to an accountant or check GOV.UK rather than guessing.

### How long does seller account verification usually take?

It varies, and Amazon does not publish a fixed timeframe. Having your documents consistent and ready, and attending the video call somewhere quiet with good lighting, are the two things most likely to keep the process moving without delays.
