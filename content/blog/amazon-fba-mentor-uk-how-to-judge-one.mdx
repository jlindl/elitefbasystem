An Amazon FBA mentor UK sellers can actually rely on is someone who still sells on Amazon themselves, gives you real scheduled time rather than a drip-feed of generic videos, and is reachable between sessions when something breaks. That is the whole test. If a mentorship cannot answer those three questions clearly, keep looking.

Most people looking into this have a 9-5 already. Stock is going to arrive at the house. Boxes get packed on a Sunday evening after everyone else has gone to bed. You are not after a hobby project, you are after something that fits around a job and does not fall over the first time a listing gets suppressed or a shipment gets rejected at the fulfilment centre. A mentor who understands that pace, and still runs their own account day to day, is worth more than one who taught full-time years ago and moved on.

## Amazon FBA mentor UK: what a 1-to-1 mentorship actually includes

A 1-to-1 mentorship is different from a course. A course is pre-recorded material you work through alone. A mentorship adds a person: someone who looks at your actual listing, your actual numbers, your actual supplier reply, and tells you what to do next.

That usually means some combination of:

- A scheduled call, ideally weekly, where you talk through what you have done since the last one and what is blocking you.
- A way to message between calls, so a suppressed listing or a rejected shipment on a Wednesday does not sit unanswered until the following week.
- Structured material (a course, templates, scripts) so you are not relying on the mentor to explain the basics from scratch every time.
- Examples from real listings and real ad campaigns, so you can see what good and bad look like rather than being told in the abstract.
- Access to other people doing the same thing, so you are not troubleshooting alone at 11pm.

## Who is the mentor, and do they still sell

This is the first thing to check, and the one people skip because the sales page looks polished either way.

Ask, or look for the answer on the site: does this person sell on Amazon right now, today, or did they sell for a while and now mainly teach? There is a real difference. Amazon changes constantly: new fee structures, new listing requirements, new ways a suppression notice gets worded, new quirks in how PPC reports display data. Someone who stopped selling two years ago is teaching you how Amazon used to work.

Jakub still runs his own Amazon FBA business alongside the mentorship: ordering inventory, refining listings, dealing with suppression notices, reading PPC reports. That is not a claim about results, it is a claim about currency. The advice you get reflects what is happening in Seller Central this month, not three years ago. You can read about how that business started, on the side of a 9-5, in [Jakub's story](/my-story).

A mentor who has stopped selling is not automatically useless. But if they have stopped, ask why, and ask how they keep their material current. If there is no good answer, treat the mentorship as a snapshot of an old version of Amazon.

## How much real time do you actually get

Vague promises of 'support' are the easiest thing to oversell and the hardest thing to check before you commit. Get specific. How often is the call? How long is it? Is it genuinely 1-to-1, or a group call with your name on the invite? Is it recorded, so you can go back to it when you are packing boxes on Sunday and half-remember something from the call but not the detail?

For someone working a 9-5, the shape of the support matters as much as the amount. A 20-minute weekly call that happens consistently is worth more than a vague offer of 'unlimited access' that in practice means a slow reply three days later. You want:

- A fixed, recurring slot, not something you have to chase to book.
- A short call, not a two-hour session you cannot fit around work and family.
- A recording, so decisions made on the call do not rely on memory.
- A realistic turnaround time for messages between calls, stated plainly rather than implied.

Elite FBA's weekly call runs around 20 minutes and is recorded, with direct messaging between calls usually answered within hours on weekdays. That is a structure built for someone fitting this around other commitments, not someone with unlimited daytime hours to spend on Amazon.

## What should be included, beyond the calls

A call on its own is not a mentorship, it is a chat. What sits around the call matters just as much.

Look for:

- **A structured course.** You should not need the mentor to explain, from a blank page, what a GTIN exemption is or how FBA shipments work. That should already be covered in material you can revisit, so the call is used for your specific situation, not for repeating basics.
- **Templates and scripts.** Supplier negotiation emails, SOPs for prepping stock, checklists for launching a listing. These save hours of trial and error, particularly for someone fitting this in around evenings and weekends.
- **Teardowns.** Seeing a real listing or a real ad campaign picked apart, with the reasoning explained, teaches you more than being told a rule in isolation.
- **A community.** Other people hitting the same problems, at a similar stage, who are awake when you are packing boxes at 9pm on a Sunday. Elite FBA includes a [private community](/#community) for exactly this.
- **No upsells.** If the mentorship keeps introducing new paid tiers once you are in, that tells you something about how it is run. Elite FBA is one programme, with lifetime updates as Amazon changes, and no upsells.

## Common mistakes people make when choosing a mentor

A few patterns come up often enough to flag directly.

**Judging on promised income.** No honest mentor can promise what you will earn. Results depend on the product, the capital you put in, the work you do and the market at the time. Anyone guaranteeing figures is not someone to trust with your money.

**Confusing a course for a mentorship.** Pre-recorded videos are useful, but they cannot look at your specific listing and tell you why it is not converting. If you only have a 9-5 evening a week to spend on this, 1-to-1 attention on your actual account is worth more than another module to watch.

**Not checking current activity.** As above: someone who stopped selling is teaching an old version of Amazon. Check this before anything else.

**Signing up before understanding the basics.** If you have not yet opened a seller account or worked out what FBA fees actually involve, start with the groundwork. Read through [how to start Amazon FBA UK as a complete beginner](/blog/how-to-start-amazon-fba-uk-complete-beginner) and [Amazon FBA fees UK explained for beginners](/blog/amazon-fba-fees-uk-explained-for-beginners) first, so a call with a mentor is spent on your product and your numbers, not on definitions.

**Assuming more hours means more support.** A mentor who is always theoretically available but slow to reply is less useful than one with a fixed weekly call and a clear turnaround time on messages.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is an Amazon FBA mentor worth it for someone with a full-time job?

It depends on what you need. If you are short on time rather than short on information, a mentor who reviews your actual listing and tells you what to fix next can save hours of guessing. If you have not yet got the basics down, work through those first so the time with a mentor is spent on your specific situation.

### How do I know if an Amazon FBA mentor is still active on Amazon?

Ask directly, or look for evidence on their site that they describe day to day selling tasks like ordering stock, managing listings or reading advertising reports in the present tense, not as something they used to do.

### What is the difference between a course and a 1-to-1 mentorship?

A course is material you work through alone. A mentorship adds scheduled time with a person who looks at your actual account and answers your specific questions, usually alongside course material rather than instead of it.

### How much does an Amazon FBA mentorship cost?

Pricing varies between mentors and depends on what is included. Ask any mentor directly what is covered for the fee, including the length and frequency of calls, support between sessions and whether there are extra charges later.

### Can I do Amazon FBA alongside a 9-5?

Yes, many people start this way, fitting product research, supplier contact and prepping stock around evenings and weekends. It takes consistent time and realistic expectations about the work involved rather than treating it as something that runs on its own.
