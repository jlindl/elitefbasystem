An Amazon GTIN exemption UK sellers can apply for through Seller Central lets you list a product without an EAN or UPC barcode, which is what most people need when they are building a bundle or multi-pack under a generic brand rather than reselling a branded product exactly as it comes off the shelf. You ask for the exemption on the listing itself, Amazon checks the category and the brand name you have chosen, and if it approves the request you can publish without ever buying a barcode for that product. It is not automatic, and not every product or category qualifies, so treat it as a step to plan for rather than something to sort out at the last minute.

If you work a 9-to-5 and are building your first listing in the evenings, this usually comes up at exactly the point you are ready to upload: you have found a product, worked out a bundle, and then Seller Central asks for a barcode you were never going to buy for a one-off generic-brand multi-pack. Knowing how the exemption works before you reach that screen saves a weekend of sitting on stock you cannot list, with boxes stacked in the hallway and nothing to show for it.

## What a GTIN exemption actually exempts you from

Every product on Amazon normally needs a global trade identification number, usually an EAN or UPC, so the catalogue can match it to the correct listing. Branded single products already have one printed on the packaging from the manufacturer. A GTIN exemption removes that requirement for cases where a proper barcode genuinely does not exist, most commonly:

- A bundle or multi-pack you have put together yourself, for example three single items from a wholesaler repackaged and sold as one unit
- A product sold under your own generic brand name rather than the manufacturer's brand
- Certain handmade or custom items that never had a manufacturer barcode to begin with

It does not exempt you from labelling the product correctly, from following any category-specific safety requirements, or from being honest about what the product actually is. It simply tells Amazon's catalogue not to expect a code that does not exist for that specific listing.

## Amazon GTIN exemption UK: how to apply through Seller Central

The request sits inside the listing creation flow, not in a separate form you fill in once. In practice it works like this:

1. Decide on the generic brand name you will use on the listing and on the packaging or polybag label. This needs to be consistent across every listing you create under it.
2. Choose the correct product category. Some categories are more likely to be approved for exemption than others, and some are gated altogether, which is a separate check you need to do regardless of the barcode question.
3. On the add a product page, select the option that tells Amazon you do not have a GTIN, rather than trying to force a code that does not belong to the product.
4. Give the reason Amazon asks for, which is normally a short explanation that this is a bundle or generic-brand product with no manufacturer barcode.
5. Upload clear images of the packaging showing your brand name and any required labels, since Amazon often asks for these before approving.
6. Submit and wait for a decision. Approval times are not fixed and can vary, so check the current guidance and any live status in Seller Central Help rather than assuming a particular turnaround.

Because Amazon reviews each request by category and brand, the same product sold under two different generic brand names, or in two different categories, will not necessarily get the same answer. Always check Amazon's current exemption rules in Seller Central Help before you plan a launch date around it.

## What Amazon checks before it approves the request

Approval is not a formality. Amazon is trying to protect the catalogue from duplicate listings, counterfeit claims and confusing search results, so the checks tend to focus on:

- Whether the category allows GTIN exemption at all. Some categories are more restricted than others.
- Whether the brand name you have chosen could be mistaken for, or infringes on, an existing registered brand or trademark. Using a name too close to a known brand is one of the fastest ways to get rejected, or worse, flagged later.
- Whether the images you submit actually show the product, the brand name and any mandatory labelling, rather than a generic stock photo.
- Whether the listing details match what you are actually selling, including pack size and contents.

If you are also including any single branded items inside a bundle alongside your own generic brand elements, remember that reselling another brand's product can need brand approval or invoices from an authorised supplier, and some categories are gated regardless of GTIN status. Check the current policy in Seller Central Help before you commit stock to that plan.

## Common mistakes sellers make with the GTIN exemption

Most rejections and hold-ups come down to a handful of repeat mistakes:

- Choosing a brand name that is too similar to an existing one, which can trigger a rejection or a later complaint from the brand owner
- Assuming the exemption applies everywhere, when certain categories either do not qualify or need extra documentation first
- Submitting photos of early samples rather than the actual finished packaging and labels the customer will receive
- Forgetting that each unit inside the bundle still needs correct labelling and any required warnings, even though the outer bundle itself has no barcode
- Not keeping supplier invoices on file, which can matter if Amazon later asks you to prove where the stock came from
- Leaving the application until stock has already arrived and is sitting in boxes at home, which turns a planning step into a bottleneck

None of this is unusual or a sign you have done something wrong. It is simply the checking process working as intended, and building it into your timeline rather than fighting it is the more reliable approach. ## What to do next if you only have evenings and weekends

If your Amazon work happens around a job, the practical order is usually:

1. Settle on your generic brand name and check it is not already in use or too close to an existing trademark, before you order any packaging or labels.
2. Apply for the GTIN exemption as part of listing creation, ideally before stock arrives, so you are not sitting on boxes you cannot list.
3. Use a small test batch rather than a full pallet, so if the listing, the exemption or the product itself needs changing, you have not committed your whole budget.
4. Set aside a specific evening or weekend slot for the polybagging, labelling and packing, since this is the part that genuinely needs your hands rather than a screen.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a GTIN exemption for every bundle I create?

You need one for any bundle or generic-brand product that does not already have a manufacturer barcode covering that exact combination of items. If you are reselling a single branded product unchanged, it usually already has its own barcode and no exemption applies.

### What happens if Amazon rejects my GTIN exemption request?

Amazon will normally give a reason, and the fix depends on what it flagged, whether that is the brand name, the category or the images submitted. Check the specific reason in Seller Central Help, correct it and reapply rather than trying the same request again unchanged.

### Does a GTIN exemption mean I own a registered brand?

No. A GTIN exemption only lets you list without a barcode under a brand name you have chosen. It does not register a trademark or give you Brand Registry protections. Those are separate steps you may choose to take later as you move from a generic reseller setup to owning a protected brand.

### Can I use the same generic brand name across different categories?

You can, but each exemption request is still reviewed by category, so approval in one category does not mean the other will automatically follow. Keep your brand name and labelling consistent regardless, since inconsistency is one of the things reviewers look for.

### Is applying for a GTIN exemption free?

Amazon does not charge a separate fee for the exemption request itself, but your usual seller account and referral fees still apply to anything you sell. Always check the current fee figures on Amazon's own fee pages or in Seller Central rather than relying on an old figure, since these change.
